        How does ELA detect manipulation?

        JPEG compression is lossy: each save introduces a roughly uniform error across the picture. ELA re-saves the image at a known quality (90 %) and amplifies the difference with the original.

        • Uniform dark/low error — regions share the same compression history (likely untouched).
        • Bright patches — regions pasted or edited from another source compress differently and stand out.
        • Edges and high-contrast textures naturally appear brighter, so ELA is an indicator to be combined with other tests.

            How it works

            Techniques used by this tool and how to interpret them.

            Metadata
            Reads EXIF tags (camera, date, GPS, software). Missing EXIF or editor signatures such as Photoshop are red flags.
            Hashing
            MD5 & SHA-256 prove bit-exact integrity. Perceptual hashes (aHash, pHash, dHash, wHash) find resized or re-compressed copies.
            ELA
            Re-compresses at 90 % and amplifies the difference. Edited regions have a different error level.
            Histogram
            Detects clipping, combing gaps and narrow tonal range produced by Levels/Curves adjustments.
            Noise
            Block-wise residual noise (Immerkaer sigma, median-filter residual) and 8×8 JPEG blocking ratio reveal inconsistent regions.
            Clone detection
            ORB keypoints are matched against themselves; RANSAC validates a consistent geometric transform = copy-move.
            Disclaimer: automated forensics gives probabilistic indicators, not legal proof. Always verify with a qualified examiner and preserve the original evidence.
